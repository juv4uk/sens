Random paths give the same qualitative result; see instructions.tsv.

Bounded findings:
1. Cold root+suffix costs ~4 fewer I-refs/call than the dense flat table
   at every measured depth in this mechanism model.
2. Prepared compact descriptors are effectively tied with cold decode in
   steady execution. The root/suffix decode itself is already tiny here.
3. Runtime HashMap caching adds about 105 I-refs/call over cold root+suffix.
4. Hybrid routing that sends generated paths through that HashMap adds about
   107 I-refs/call at depth > 0. Root-only hybrid stays on the direct arm.
5. Flat-table preparation grows with address-space depth; at depth 16 it
   costs about 2.2M extra I-refs over cold preparation even for one repeated
   word because the full table is materialized.
6. Preparation-heavy "compiled descriptor" in this standalone harness is NOT
   CML direct native lowering. CML #395 must test whether lowering the known
   suffix into direct IR/native instructions removes the path loop itself.

Semantic interpretation:
- this is mechanism evidence, not a new semantic authority;
- semantic-node count is unchanged;
- root+suffix can remain direct without a runtime hash cache;
- caches/tables remain disposable implementation projections;
- residue lookup was deliberately zero for this proven selector-only corpus.

Decision pressure:
Prefer direct root+suffix for the interpreter baseline unless later SENS-runtime
measurements contradict this mechanism result. For the compiler, do not merely
cache a decoded descriptor: test compiling the path away into direct primitive
chains/native instructions.

Raw evidence and provenance are in this directory.
